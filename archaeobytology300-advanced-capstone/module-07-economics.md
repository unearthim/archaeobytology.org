**Duration**: 1 week  
**Type**: Strategy & Financial Planning

## Module Overview

**How do you fund digital sovereignty without selling out to surveillance capitalism?**

In this module, you'll explore alternative economic models for sustaining digital commons. You'll analyze patronage, cooperatives, public funding, and hybrid models—then design a viable financial strategy for a sovereignty project.

---

## Learning Objectives

1. **Analyze funding models**: Understand strengths and weaknesses of different approaches
2. **Design hybrid strategies**: Combine multiple revenue streams for resilience
3. **Navigate tradeoffs**: Recognize when economic necessities conflict with sovereignty principles
4. **Plan realistically**: Create budgets and financial projections that could actually work
5. **Think systemically**: See how funding shapes governance, access, and sustainability

### Part 2: Hybrid Funding Strategy (Due: End of Week)

**Design a complete funding strategy** for a digital sovereignty project (real or hypothetical).

**Choose a project type**:
- Federated social network (Mastodon-like)
- Digital preservation archive
- Sovereignty tool or service
- Community infrastructure

**Your funding strategy document (5-7 pages) must include**:

**1. Revenue Streams**

Design a **hybrid model** with at least 3 revenue sources:

**Possible streams**:
- Individual donations (one-time or recurring)
- Membership tiers (benefits for supporters)
- Institutional grants (foundations, government)
- Cooperative ownership (user-members pay in)
- Service revenue (hosting, consulting, training)
- Corporate sponsorship (with clear ethical boundaries)
- Public funding (library budgets, municipal support)

For each stream:
- How much could it realistically generate?
- What are the strings attached?
- How stable is it?

**2. Budget Breakdown**

Create detailed first-year budget:
- Infrastructure costs (servers, storage, bandwidth)
- Personnel (if any paid staff)
- Operations (legal, accounting, insurance)
- Community and outreach
- Reserve fund (recommended: 6-12 months operating costs)

**Total**: What's your annual budget need?

**3. 5-Year Financial Projection**

Show how revenue grows (or doesn't):
- Year 1: Startup phase
- Year 2-3: Growth phase
- Year 4-5: Mature operations

Be realistic about:
- How long does it take to build donation base?
- When do grants run out?
- What happens during economic downturns?

**4. Governance & Accountability**

How does funding shape decision-making?
- Do donors get voting power?
- How do you prevent capture by large funders?
- What's your transparency policy?
- How do you handle conflicts of interest?

**5. Ethical Boundaries**

What money will you NOT take?
- Surveillance companies?
- Extractive corporations?
- Government agencies with poor human rights records?
- Conditional funding that undermines mission?

Write clear ethical guidelines for funding decisions.

**6. Sustainability & Risk**

**Risk analysis**:
- What if your largest funder leaves?
- What if donations drop 50%?
- What if costs balloon (infrastructure, legal)?

**Mitigation strategies**:
- Diversification (multiple revenue streams)
- Reserve funds
- Graceful degradation plans
- Community ownership (not dependent on founders)

**Deliverable**: Complete funding strategy (5-7 pages) + budget spreadsheet

## Case Study Deep Dives

### Success Stories

**Wikipedia/Wikimedia Foundation**:
- $180M annual budget (2023)
- 100% donation-funded (no ads)
- Banner campaigns during funding drives
- Endowment for long-term stability
- **Lesson**: Massive scale + strong mission = donation viability

**Signal Foundation**:
- ~$40M/year operating costs
- Funded by Brian Acton ($50M initial) + donations
- No ads, no data collection, completely private
- **Lesson**: Patient capital + user trust = sustainability
- **Challenge**: What happens when founder funding ends?

**Mastodon gGmbH (the nonprofit behind Mastodon)**:
- ~$50K/month Patreon support
- Grants from NGI, NLnet
- One full-time developer (Eugen)
- **Lesson**: Small budgets CAN sustain important infrastructure
- **Challenge**: Scaling up without compromising sovereignty

### Cautionary Tales

**Ello** (2014-2015):
- Raised $5.5M VC funding as "anti-Facebook"
- Promised: no ads, no data collection
- Reality: VCs expect 10x returns
- **Lesson**: VC funding and sovereignty are incompatible

**Diaspora** (2010-2015):
- Crowdfunded $200K+ initially
- Failed to build sustainable model
- Developers burned out, project languished
- **Lesson**: Initial funding ≠ long-term sustainability

**App.net** (2012-2014):
- Subscription model: $5/month per user
- Needed 100K paying users to be viable
- Got 20K, couldn't sustain
- **Lesson**: Paid social media faces adoption challenges
