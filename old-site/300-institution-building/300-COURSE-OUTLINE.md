# Archaeobytology 300: Institution Building & Strategic Infrastructure
**Course Subtitle:** From Personal Practice to Public Systems  
**Level:** Advanced Graduate Seminar  
**Prerequisites:** Archaeobytology 101 (Theory) and 200 (Practice)

---

## Course Description

The 101-level series established the foundational theory of Archaeobytology—the vocabulary and conceptual framework for understanding digital artifacts. The 200-level course taught hands-on methodology—how to excavate, classify, preserve, and forge these artifacts as an individual practitioner.

The 300-level course addresses the discipline's ultimate challenge: **How do we build institutions, organizations, and movements that embody these principles and survive beyond individual practitioners?**

This is Archaeobytology as applied strategy. Students will design preservation organizations, sovereign business models, memory institutions, and movement-building frameworks. The goal is not just to practice Archaeobytology—but to create the infrastructure that ensures the discipline (and its principles) endure for generations.

---

## Core Thesis

**"The Internet Archive is one flood away from losing 800 billion pages. GeoCities was deleted by corporate decree. The Fediverse fragments across volunteer servers. Web3 promised sovereignty but delivered speculation.**

**The 200-level taught you to work as an individual Archaeobytologist. The 300-level asks: What institutions must we build to ensure this work—and The Three Pillars—survive the next 50 years?"**

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Analyze** existing digital preservation institutions for their alignment with The Three Pillars (Declaration, Connection, Ground)
2. **Design** sustainable business models for Archive and Anvil work that don't rely on platform rent-seeking
3. **Architect** technical and social systems for decentralized, resilient digital infrastructure
4. **Build** movement-building strategies to transform Archaeobytology from practice to recognized discipline
5. **Create** policy frameworks and public scholarship that translate technical work into civic impact
6. **Synthesize** a complete institutional design (organization, business model, governance, technical architecture)

## Course Structure: Eight Modules

### **Module 0: The Institutional Void**
**Reading:** "The Brittleness of Digital Memory" — Why Individual Practice Isn't Enough

**Core Question:** What happens when the Archaeobytologist retires, gets acquired, or runs out of funding?

**Case Studies:**
- The Internet Archive's single-point-of-failure problem (San Francisco building)
- Yahoo's deletion of GeoCities (centralized control)
- The fragmentation of Mastodon (decentralized but unstable)
- Web3's failure to deliver on sovereignty promises

**Output:** Students identify one "institutional failure mode" and propose a structural solution.

---

### **Module 1: The Business of the Archive**
**Reading:** Kahle, "Preserving the Internet"; Benkler, "The Wealth of Networks"

**Core Question:** What are sustainable business models for preservation work that embody sovereign Ground ownership?

**Case Studies:**
- Internet Archive (non-profit + digital lending)
- Software Heritage (academic consortium)
- Perma.cc (university library collaboration)
- unearth.im (for-profit brand foundry)
- Archive Team (volunteer collective)

**Framework:** The Archive Sustainability Matrix
- Funding Models: Grants, Donations, Memberships, Revenue
- Governance: Non-profit, Co-op, DAO, For-profit, Academic
- Technical Infrastructure: Centralized, Federated, P2P, Blockchain
- Alignment with Three Pillars: Declaration (autonomy), Connection (community), Ground (ownership)

**Assignment:** Design a preservation organization with a 10-year sustainability plan. Must answer:
1. How is it funded?
2. Who owns/governs it?
3. Where is the data stored (and who controls it)?
4. How does it embody The Three Pillars?

---

### **Module 2: The Economics of the Anvil**
**Reading:** Pye, "The Nature and Art of Workmanship"; Doctorow, "Enshittification"

**Core Question:** How do you monetize sovereignty in an economy optimized for platform rent-seeking?

**Case Studies:**
- unearth.im's domain portfolio strategy (selling sovereign Ground)
- Metalabel (artist sovereignty infrastructure)
- Ghost (escape from Medium/Substack)
- Buttondown (indie newsletter platform)
- Roam Research → Obsidian migration (local-first tooling)

**Framework:** The Anvil Business Canvas
- Value Proposition: What does sovereignty provide that platforms can't?
- Revenue Streams: One-time (domains), Recurring (consulting), Hybrid (monuments-as-service)
- Customer Segments: Who pays for sovereignty? (Founders, Institutions, Movements)
- Key Resources: Portfolio (curated assets), Methodology (frameworks), Proof-of-work (monuments)

**Assignment:** Design a "foundry" business model. Choose one Forging Act (Portfolio, Monuments, Frameworks) and create:
1. Service offering description
2. Pricing model (how sovereignty is valued)
3. Client journey (how they move from platform to Ground)
4. 3-year financial projection

---

### **Module 3: Building the Seed Bank (Collaborative Preservation)**
**Reading:** Gitelman, "'Raw Data' Is an Oxymoron"; Lessig, "Code 2.0"

**Core Question:** How do you scale preservation beyond heroic individuals to collaborative infrastructure?

**Case Studies:**
- Archive Team's distributed scraping model
- Software Heritage's university consortium
- LOCKSS ("Lots of Copies Keep Stuff Safe")
- IPFS (InterPlanetary File System)
- Filecoin's economic incentive layer

**Framework:** The Seed Bank Design Matrix
- Coordination: How do distributed preservers collaborate?
- Verification: How do we ensure data integrity?
- Incentives: Why do people/orgs contribute storage/bandwidth?
- Access: Who can retrieve preserved artifacts?
- Governance: Who decides what to preserve?

**Assignment:** Design a collaborative Seed Bank for a specific domain (e.g., "personal homepages," "indie games," "underground zines"). Must include:
1. Technical architecture (storage, replication, retrieval)
2. Social architecture (contributor onboarding, governance)
3. Economic model (how is it funded/sustained?)
4. Ethics framework (what preservation biases exist?)

---

### **Module 4: Curating the Haunted Forest (Memory Institutions)**
**Reading:** Turkle, "Evocative Objects"; Parikka, "What is Media Archaeology?"

**Core Question:** How do you create public-facing institutions that honor murdered platforms and extinct ecosystems?

**Case Studies:**
- One Terabyte of Kilobyte Age (GeoCities art project)
- The Museum of Obsolete Media (physical archive)
- Cameron's World (GeoCities aesthetic preservation)
- Rhizome's Net Art Anthology
- The Strong Museum's Video Game History collection

**Framework:** The Haunted Forest Exhibition Model
- Curation Strategy: What stories do Umbrabytes tell?
- Access Design: How do visitors experience ghosts? (Read-only mirror? Interactive emulation?)
- Context Restoration: How do you explain lost ecosystems to modern audiences?
- Ethical Stance: How do you honor communities who lost their Ground?

**Assignment:** Design a digital museum/memorial for one murdered platform. Must include:
1. Curatorial statement (what is the story?)
2. Exhibition design (how do visitors experience it?)
3. Contextual materials (explainers, timelines, oral histories)
4. Ethical framework (how do you handle private data? Lost communities?)

---

### **Module 5: The Political Economy of Ground Ownership**
**Reading:** Zuboff, "Surveillance Capitalism"; Véliz, "Privacy Is Power"; Berners-Lee, "Long Live the Web"

**Core Question:** What are the technical, legal, and political paths to reclaiming digital sovereignty?

**Case Studies:**
- Domain names (ICANN, centralized control)
- ENS (Ethereum Name Service, blockchain domains)
- Handshake (decentralized DNS)
- Indie Web (own your domain + POSSE strategy)
- Mastodon/ActivityPub (federated identity)
- DID (Decentralized Identifiers) standards

**Framework:** The Ground Sovereignty Stack
- Layer 1: Physical Infrastructure (who owns the servers?)
- Layer 2: Protocol (open standards vs. proprietary APIs)
- Layer 3: Identity (who controls usernames/domains?)
- Layer 4: Data (who owns the content?)
- Layer 5: Social (who governs the rules?)

**Assignment:** Design a complete sovereignty system for one use case (e.g., "scholarly publishing," "artist portfolios," "local news"). Must address all 5 layers and answer:
1. How do users claim their Ground?
2. How is it technically secured against landlords?
3. How is it economically sustainable?
4. How does it resist capture/enshittification?

---

### **Module 6: Movement Building (From Practice to Discipline)**
**Reading:** Kuhn, "The Structure of Scientific Revolutions"; Parikka, "Media Archaeology" (field formation)

**Core Question:** How do you transform a practice into a recognized academic/professional discipline?

**Case Studies:**
- How "Media Archaeology" became a field (1990s-2010s)
- How "User Experience Design" professionalized (2000s)
- How "Digital Humanities" secured institutional legitimacy
- How "Surveillance Studies" emerged post-Snowden
- How "Platform Studies" organized around critical infrastructure

**Framework:** The Discipline Formation Playbook
- **Phase 1: Intellectual Foundation** (books, essays, neologisms)
- **Phase 2: Community Building** (conferences, workshops, email lists)
- **Phase 3: Institutional Legitimation** (academic departments, journals, grants)
- **Phase 4: Professional Infrastructure** (certifications, job titles, consulting market)

**Assignment:** Design a 10-year movement-building strategy for Archaeobytology. Choose 3 tactics from each phase and create:
1. Timeline with milestones
2. Budget and funding sources
3. Key partnerships (universities, archives, foundations)
4. Success metrics (how do you know it's working?)

---

### **Module 7: The Archaeobytologist as Public Intellectual**
**Reading:** Small, "The Public Value of the Humanities"; Zuboff's op-eds; Lanier's manifestos

**Core Question:** How do you translate technical scholarship into public discourse and policy impact?

**Case Studies:**
- How Shoshana Zuboff made "surveillance capitalism" a household term
- How Jaron Lanier influenced platform critique
- How Tim Berners-Lee's "Contract for the Web" shaped policy
- How danah boyd's teen research influenced platform design debates
- How Cory Doctorow's "enshittification" framed the moment

**Framework:** The Public Scholar Toolkit
- **Translation:** Academic → Accessible (without dumbing down)
- **Platforms:** Where do you publish? (Op-eds, podcasts, X/Bluesky, Substack)
- **Narratives:** What stories make abstract concepts concrete?
- **Coalitions:** Who are your allies? (Journalists, policymakers, activists)
- **Timing:** How do you respond to "teachable moments"?

**Assignment:** Create a public-facing intervention based on Archaeobytology principles. Choose one:
1. Op-ed responding to a recent platform extinction event
2. Policy proposal for digital preservation funding
3. Manifesto for a "Third Way" web
4. Public lecture script for non-technical audience
5. Podcast episode explaining Three Pillars to mainstream listeners

---

### **Module 8: Forging the Third Way (Post-Platform Futures)**
**Reading:** Benkler, "The Wealth of Networks"; Schneier, "Click Here to Kill Everybody"; Doctorow, "How to Destroy Surveillance Capitalism"

**Core Question:** What does a web that embodies The Three Pillars actually look like—technically, economically, and socially?

**Case Studies:**
- Mastodon/Fediverse (federated social, flawed execution)
- Bluesky/ATProtocol (algorithmic choice, sovereign identity)
- Solid (Tim Berners-Lee's data pod vision)
- IPFS + Filecoin (distributed storage + economics)
- Indie Web (domain + microformats + webmentions)
- Local-first software (Ink & Switch)

**Framework:** The Third Way Design Principles
- **Declaration:** Sovereign identity not tied to corporate platforms
- **Connection:** Intentional, algorithm-optional social graphs
- **Ground:** User-owned data, portable across services
- **Sustainability:** Economic models that don't require surveillance
- **Governance:** Democratic/co-op structures not dictatorships

**Assignment (Final Capstone):** Design a complete "Third Way" system. Choose one domain:
- Social networking
- Content publishing
- Knowledge management
- Creative collaboration
- Local community organizing

Must include:
1. **Vision Document** (what problem does this solve?)
2. **Technical Architecture** (how does it work?)
3. **Business Model** (how is it sustained?)
4. **Governance Model** (who makes decisions?)
5. **Migration Strategy** (how do people leave platforms?)
6. **10-Year Roadmap** (phases from prototype to infrastructure)

## Course Philosophy

This is not a theoretical seminar. Every assignment produces **actionable institutional design**.

Students will leave with:
- A portfolio of designed organizations (Archive, Anvil, Seed Bank, Museum)
- A movement-building strategy for Archaeobytology
- A public-facing publication (op-ed, manifesto, or policy brief)
- A complete "Third Way" system ready for prototyping

**The goal:** By the end of this course, you should be able to start a preservation non-profit, launch a sovereign foundry business, curate a digital museum, or lead a decentralization movement.

This is Archaeobytology as **discipline-building practice**. We're not just studying institutions—we're designing the ones that will carry this work into the future.
