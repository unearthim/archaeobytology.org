**Duration**: 1 week  
**Type**: Community Organizing & Strategic Communication

## Module Overview

**Digital sovereignty isn't just a technical problem—it's a political movement.**

In this module, you'll learn to organize communities around preservation and sovereignty. You'll develop outreach strategies, build coalitions, and create materials that translate archaeobytological principles for non-technical audiences.

---

## Learning Objectives

1. **Organize communities**: Build coalitions around preservation and sovereignty
2. **Communicate effectively**: Translate technical concepts for diverse audiences
3. **Design campaigns**: Create strategic interventions that shift power
4. **Navigate conflicts**: Handle disagreements within movements
5. **Build power**: Understand how social movements create change

## Assignment: Organize a Campaign

### Part 1: Movement Analysis (Due: Day 2)

**Study a successful digital rights campaign**:

**Choose one**:
- SOPA/PIPA protests (2012)
- Net Neutrality campaigns (2014-2018)
- EU Article 13 (copyright directive) opposition
- Apple CSAM scanning opposition (2021)
- Right to Repair movement

Write 3-4 pages analyzing:
- **Strategy**: What tactics did they use?
- **Coalition**: Who allied? Who was missing?
- **Communication**: How did they frame the issue?
- **Power**: What leverage did they have?
- **Outcomes**: What changed? What didn't?
- **Lessons**: What would you do differently?

**Deliverable**: Campaign analysis

---

### Part 2: Build Your Own Campaign (Due: End of Week)

**Design and launch a real organizing campaign** around digital preservation or sovereignty.

**Campaign Options**:

**Option A: Campus/Community Campaign**
- Organize digital literacy workshops
- Create preservation events ("Save Your Digital Life Day")
- Build local preservation infrastructure
- Advocate for institutional policy changes

**Option B: Online Movement Building**
- Create educational content series (social media, blog, video)
- Build online community around sovereignty
- Coordinate collective action (migration from Big Tech platform)
- Crowdfund preservation project

**Option C: Policy Advocacy**
- Draft institutional preservation policy
- Advocate for library funding for digital archives
- Push for open protocol requirements in government systems
- Support digital rights legislation

**Your campaign packet (5-7 pages) must include**:

**1. Campaign Goals**
- What are you trying to change?
- What does success look like?
- What's your theory of change?

**2. Target Audiences**
- Who do you need to reach?
- What do they care about?
- How do you reach them?

**3. Key Messages**
- Core talking points (3-5 key messages)
- Different framings for different audiences
- Responses to likely objections

**4. Tactics & Timeline**
- What actions will you take?
- What's the sequence?
- What resources do you need?

**5. Coalition Building**
- Who are your allies?
- How will you coordinate?
- How will you handle conflicts?

**6. Communication Materials**

Create at least THREE pieces of campaign content:
- Social media graphics
- One-pager explaining the issue
- Email/petition copy
- Video script
- Presentation slides
- Zine or infographic

**7. Evaluation Plan**
- How will you measure success?
- What metrics matter?
- How will you adapt if tactics aren't working?

**Deliverable**: 
- Campaign plan (5-7 pages)
- Communication materials (3+ pieces)
- Evidence of actual campaign launch (screenshots, documentation)

## Assessment Rubric

### Movement Analysis (30 points)
- **Strategic insight** (15 pts): Deep understanding of campaign tactics
- **Critical analysis** (10 pts): Identifies strengths and weaknesses
- **Application** (5 pts): Draws lessons for your own organizing

### Campaign Design (70 points)
- **Strategic clarity** (20 pts): Clear goals and theory of change
- **Audience understanding** (15 pts): Messages tailored to targets
- **Communication quality** (20 pts): Materials are compelling and clear
- **Feasibility** (10 pts): Campaign is realistic and achievable
- **Actual launch** (5 pts): Evidence you actually started organizing
